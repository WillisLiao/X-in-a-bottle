# 2026-07-30

## Research

Analysed three Reddit captures to work out what actually travels and what the quality bar is.

**r/iosapps, top of month.** 56k weekly visitors, and only five app posts cleared 100 upvotes.
Winners: fog-of-war walking app (187), ad-free weather app (139), hand-drawn "doors to quiet worlds" (127), travel history reconstructed from photo library (126), GeoSpoof (110).
The gap between 110 and the next post at 59 is the real signal.

Shared traits: latent on-device data giving an instant payoff with zero setup, the physical world as input, explicit anti-app framing (ad-free, offline, no algorithms), visible human craft, and a single screenshot that explains itself.
Comment counts running roughly 1.5x upvotes on the winners, meaning the posts started arguments.

**r/appledevelopers.** 22k weekly visitors.
Every post over 100 upvotes is a screenshot of a revenue dashboard: £1k first month (175), game revenue during infancy (166), first sale (137), first month (133).
None of them describe a product.
Scale is not the variable either - 20 downloads got 27 upvotes and a $60 payout got 9.
What travels is a real number attached to an honest feeling.

Most useful single post found: "11 years of maintaining the same iOS app" (DayGram, 31 upvotes).
Three claims from it, backed by a decade of evidence, that we are adopting directly.
Platform churn costs more than features.
Saying no is the actual work.
Data responsibility is a feature, not an afterthought.

Also consistent across all three captures: AI-framed posts get nothing.

## Concepts considered and dropped

- **Terra Incognita.** Hand-engraved atlas revealed by walking, seeded from photo library GPS. Dropped: collided almost line for line with PixGlobe (found unlaunched in the r/appledevelopers capture, same on-device stance, same vintage atlas theme, $6.99), plus the 126-upvote travel app and both Photos Maps incumbents. Four players, one lane.
- **Shoebox.** Turning the utility half of a camera roll into a searchable archive. Sound analysis, rejected on taste. Worth noting the "PDF parsing ate two weeks" post corroborated that reliable offline extraction is a real moat, if this ever comes back.
- **Intergenerational co-op game** matching younger players with isolated elderly players. Emotionally the strongest option and genuinely important, dropped on risk: the failure mode is a scammer reaching an 88-year-old through your app, which demands a vetting operation rather than a solo build.

## Decided

**Lull.** See `handoffs/HANDOFF.md`.

Chose it because the anti-app stance is the mechanic rather than the marketing, it starts the argument that the high-comment posts all started, and the accessibility story is structural (no reading, no precision, no timing) rather than bolted on.

## Stack

Question was Godot, Unity or Unreal.
For iOS alone the answer was neither - native Metal plus SwiftUI, because the visual is one fragment shader, the hard problem is banding and colour management in near-black on OLED, and power is a product requirement rather than an optimisation.

Target changed to iOS and Android at launch, which flips it to **Godot 4.4+**.
Mobile renderer, Metal driver on iOS, Vulkan on Android.
Unity would charge engine weight and licensing for systems this game does not use.
Unreal is wrong on size and thermals for something played in bed.

Accepted costs of the Godot decision, recorded so they are not rediscovered later.
Accessibility stops being free, so text-bearing screens should live in native views around the Godot canvas.
Every system feature (billing, Focus status, brightness, sleep schedule) becomes a plugin.
Android panel variance will break near-black gradients that look perfect on iPhone, so the dither must be robust rather than tuned, and cheap Android hardware is needed on the desk early.

## Stack, reversed

Target changed back to **iOS only**, which reverts the decision to the original recommendation: native Metal plus SwiftUI, no engine.
Godot scaffold was deleted and the two pieces worth keeping, the decay curve and the shader, were ported.

Recording the churn because the answer genuinely depends on the platform target and it flipped twice.
iOS only means native. iOS plus Android means Godot. Deciding late means a rewrite.

## Built

- `Lull/Decay.swift` - one curve, twenty minute session, driving luminance, saturation, time scale and target framerate together. Owns let-go detection and the fade.
- `Lull/Shaders/Drift.metal` - drifting fbm field, warm light, vignette falling to true black for OLED, interleaved gradient noise dither before quantisation, sRGB to linear conversion for the palette.
- `Lull/Renderer.swift`, `Lull/DriftView.swift`, `Lull/Support/ShaderTypes.h`, `Lull/LullApp.swift`.
- `project.yml` for xcodegen, so `Lull.xcodeproj` is disposable.

Builds clean, zero warnings.

## Bugs found by actually running it

Three, none of which would have been caught by reading the code.

**Fullscreen triangle UV mapping was at half scale.** The triangle deliberately overshoots to cover the screen with three vertices, so `p` spans 0 to 2 and NDC spans -1 to 3, with the visible screen only being the `p = 0..1` part. UV was being derived as `p * 0.5`, which meant the visible screen only covered `uv.x` 0 to 0.5 and `uv.y` 0.5 to 1. A light at (0.5, 0.5) therefore rendered in the top-right corner. First screenshot looked like a vertical orange ramp, which is what a Gaussian looks like when you can only see a quarter of it.

**Light was roughly seven times too bright.** The warm colour was being added at full strength, peaking near 1.0 linear. Added a `lightIntensity` uniform and set it to 0.14. A night app looked at in a dark room cannot approach 1.0 or it reads as a lamp.

**`MTKView.colorspace` is macOS only.** On iOS it has to be set on the underlying `CAMetalLayer`.

Also cleared up Swift 6 actor isolation: `init` is `@MainActor`, `draw(in:)` asserts main isolation via `MainActor.assumeIsolated` since MTKView drives it on the main thread, and `simd_clamp` replaced a hand-rolled clamp that was shadowing `Swift.min`.

## The fog was invisible on device, and the fix was not what I expected

Three attempts, two of them wrong for interesting reasons.

First guess was that the palette was too dark, so `kMidColor` went up roughly
3x. Real improvement, did not fix it.
Second guess was that contrast was compressed, so the field got
`smoothstep(0.28, 0.72)` applied. Also a real improvement, also did not fix it.

Rendering the field alone with the ember disabled and measuring pixels settled
it. The field was never broken and was never too dark: spread was 28/255 with a
stdev of 5.86. The problem was **spatial frequency**. The base octave at `p * 2.2`
over a domain 0.46 wide gave under one cycle across the screen, and a gradient
that gradual sits below the eye's contrast sensitivity, which is already poor at
low spatial frequency and much worse in the dark. The ember, eight times
brighter in linear terms, held all the dark adaptation.

Raising the base to `p * 7.0` and tripling the drift rate took it to about four
cycles down the screen, measured by counting mean crossings in a vertical
profile. Visible on device immediately.

Lesson worth keeping: for anything near black, measure before tuning. Two
plausible diagnoses in a row were both wrong and both produced improvements,
which is the worst possible failure mode because it looks like progress.

## Restructured into two apps

Bottle became a separate app rather than a variant of Lull, which resolved the
tension between lightning and sleep by not having to resolve it.

Added `Shared/`: `MetalCanvas` owns device, queue, pipeline, frame loop, the
extended-linear P3 drawable and touch plumbing, and `CanvasCommon.h` owns the
fullscreen triangle, sRGB to linear, the dither and the noise. Renderers are now
logic plus a uniform struct. This is specifically the set of things already got
wrong once, so writing them twice was not an option.

## Bottle became a focus app

The idea that made it a product rather than a toy: the storm is paid for with
stillness. Leave the phone alone and lightning comes more often and reaches
further. Touch the screen or move the phone and it drains, twelve times faster
than it fills.

The asymmetry is the mechanism. Loss aversion is what makes leaving the phone
alone feel like something, and a symmetric version would just be a timer.

`Charge` holds the level, `MotionMonitor` reads `userAcceleration` via CoreMotion
(gravity already removed, no authorisation required, so the app asks for
nothing), and `Storm` reads charge for both strike interval and bolt reach.

Touch was previously an interaction that leaned the next bolt toward the finger.
Removed: it cannot be both a reward and a punishment. Touching is now only ever
a cost.

Verified in the simulator with fill time cut to 10 seconds. Empty reads as a
dark empty vessel at luma 11.27. Full reads as a lit storm with a bolt through
it, 14.20 at rest and 51.48 during a strike.

## Bolt timing was physically right and practically wrong

The bolt was set to 0.16s, which is defensible for real lightning and meant it
was never once seen: at 60fps it occupied a handful of frames, and every capture
caught only the 0.85s afterglow. Raised to 0.30s with a flicker riding on the
decay envelope, since real lightning restrikes down the same channel and that is
why it appears to flutter rather than switch off.

Also raised the midpoint-displacement offset from 0.05 to 0.085 and slowed its
decay from 0.5 to 0.62, because at the textbook values the bolt was smooth
enough to read as a crack in the glass rather than lightning inside it.

## Bottle became "X in a Bottle", and the phone became the bottle

Two changes, and the second one is the better idea.

Dropping the drawn bottle was correct on its own terms: the glass SDF with its
lit rim was reading as a neon sign rather than a vessel, and every attempt to
tone it down made it read as a painted shape instead. Making the phone the
bottle removes the problem rather than tuning it, and full-screen lightning is
immediately more dramatic than lightning in a jar.

Swipeable environments turned one app into a shape that holds many. Lightning
free, Ice and Genies paid, freemium by non-consumable IAP. Worth recording that
this does not contradict the earlier no-subscription decision: the rule is no
recurring billing, not no money.

Architecture: `Environment` protocol, one world per file, `BottleRenderer` owns
the charge and the motion sensing and delegates to whichever is active.
`CanvasCoordinator` now caches pipelines by fragment function name and builds
them on demand, so swiping does not stall on shader compilation.

## What each environment does with the same two numbers

Charge and disturbance are shared. Every world reads them in its own idiom,
which is what makes the set feel like one app rather than three toys.

- Lightning: charge is strike frequency and how far a bolt reaches; disturbance
  is the storm knocked flat and the next strike pushed back.
- Ice: charge is how many crystals are entitled to grow and how far; disturbance
  is a thaw at ten times the growth rate, with a wet sheen across the ice.
- Genies: charge is population; disturbance is an earthquake that shakes the
  whole world and sends 28% of them fleeing.

Deliberately not all of them flee. Losing the lot would make a disturbance feel
like a reset rather than a cost, and the cost is the product.

## Two shader shapes that came out wrong the obvious way

**Ice looked like flowers.** Modulating a circle's radius by `cos(6θ)` is the
obvious way to get six-fold symmetry and it produces fat rounded lobes: petals,
not frost. Folding the angle into a wedge and putting a narrow gaussian across
it gives arms that taper to points. Added barbs branching near the middle and a
small hard hexagonal core, on the principle that ice needs at least one edge you
could cut yourself on when everything else on screen is soft.

**Genies looked like smears.** The shaping transform stretched them 2.6x
horizontally compressed and 1.4x pinched, which at that magnitude is a vertical
blur rather than a figure. Reduced, and split into a tight bright heart inside a
softer body so there is something to focus on.

The genie art is still placeholder and should be treated as such. The behaviour
is real; the character is not designed.

## Known unfinished

The drifting field is invisible: the void and mid palette colours land around 0.001 to 0.004 linear, which is black for practical purposes.
Light radius, intensity and dither amount were all tuned by eye in a simulator that cannot represent low-brightness OLED.
None of these are worth fiddling with further until it has been looked at on a real phone in a dark room.
