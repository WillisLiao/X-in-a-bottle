# 2026-08-01 - Progress stops going backwards, and they take a break

Four changes, and between them they replace the cost model the app has run on
since it was called Bottle.

## Nothing that has been built ever comes apart

The old rule was that a disturbance knocked the last two works off the top, on
the principle that what was built can be lost.

That rule was written for a bottle that filled in fifteen minutes and emptied in
one, and against a build that takes a week it is simply wrong.
It means a phone call can erase an evening, and the rational response to a
mechanic that can erase an evening is to stop opening the app.
Losing an hour of work does not make somebody concentrate harder, it makes them
close the thing.

So `_shrink` no longer touches the queue.
Moving the phone still sends some of the crew away, and because the crew takes
fifteen unbroken minutes to come back up to strength, a disturbance still costs
real hours of building - it just costs them **forwards**, by slowing the work
down, rather than backwards by undoing it.

Progress only ever goes one way now.
That is a much better promise to make to somebody a week into a house.

Touching the screen was already free, so between the two of them the only thing
the app takes from you is time, and never work.

## An hour on, a quarter of an hour off

Every hour of *building* - not of wall clock, so it survives being put down -
they all stop for fifteen minutes.

The break runs down only while the app is open on that island.
Not while you are on the menu, not while you are on another island, not while
the phone is in your pocket.

It is worth being clear about why that is not a trick to keep the app open.
Every other timer in this genre runs down while you are away, so the correct play
is always to leave.
This one runs down only while you are here, and there is deliberately nothing to
do while it does: the elves have stopped, there is no work to watch, and the only
thing on screen is a light crossing the sky.
It is fifteen minutes of the app explicitly having nothing for you.
Take the break with them and they are ready when you get back; don't, and they
are still sitting there when you return.

Movement during the break costs nothing at all.
They have stopped anyway, and charging somebody for picking their phone up during
a rest would turn the rest into a second shift.

**How they rest** goes through the same traits as everything else, because twelve
elves doing one thing at once would undo all the individuality the rest of the
code is spent on.
The tired lie down, the sociable sit at the fire, the solitary walk off to the
spot that is theirs, the playful start a game, and somebody is always at the
water.
Sleeping is drawn sideways rather than on the back: the rig pivots at the feet,
so a quarter turn about Z lays an elf out in profile with its boots where it was
standing, and a profile is the only view of a lying figure that reads as asleep
rather than as fallen over.
Which side depends on the elf, so a dozen of them round a fire are not a row of
identical dominoes.

Sleep is weighted by how far through the break it is, which turned out to matter
much more than expected.
Weighted on tiredness alone, nobody ever lay down at all - a crew that has just
stopped working is not tired yet, so the term never won, and the whole break was
twelve elves fishing.
Weighted on time-into-the-break as well, a quarter of an hour gets a shape:

```
  5% into the break: fish 6, sit 3, ball 2, away 1
 35% into the break: fish 6, ball 3, sit 2, away 1
 70% into the break: sleep 6, fish 5, sit 1
 95% into the break: sleep 11, sit 1
```

They knock off and go straight to the water or start a game; they end it asleep,
with one still sitting up. The island goes quiet a minute or two before they
wake, which is the right note to hand the hour back on.

## The light crossing the sky

The brief asked for a rest progress bar in the sky, and this is one - but a bar
with a figure beside it would be the only countdown in an app that has worked
hard at not having any, and a countdown is something you watch rather than
something you rest through.

So it is a hairline arc from one side of the sky to the other with a warm light
travelling along it, the traversed part lit and the rest almost invisible.
It says exactly how much of the night is left without ever being a number you
could try to beat.
Underneath it the key light comes down to 42%, the fill goes cold, and the hearth
is left as the brightest thing on the island - which is the right thing for a
fire to be when everybody has gone to sit round it.

One line of text, "They have stopped for a while.", fades in for the first six
seconds of a break and then never appears again.
After that, a dozen elves lying down explains itself.

## Build time in the picker

Each island now carries a clock under its phase: `0:05:20`.

Time **spent**, never time remaining.
A countdown invites you to wait it out; a clock that only moves while you are
actually building is a record of what you did.
It excludes the menu, the other four islands, and the fifteen minutes an hour
they spend sitting down, so it is the honest answer to "how much of this did I
do" - which over a week is the only number worth keeping.

## Two bugs

**`Sky` is a native Godot class.** Naming an inner class that produces
`Parse Error: Class "Sky" hides a native class`, `main.gd` then fails to load
entirely, and the app runs as an empty scene that never quits - which presents as
a screen capture hanging forever with no error on stdout. Renamed `NightArc`.

**`--yaw` and `--zoom` did nothing.** They were parsed before `_enter`, which
resets the camera to the island default. Held apart now, so a capture that asks
for an angle gets it. Worth having: the close view at `--zoom=0.44` is the first
time the three-storey frame has been seen properly, and it is the best thing in
the app.

## Still open

- The full cycle - build, hour, break, wake, build - has been verified in
  fragments but not once end to end in a single run.
- The arc is a 2D overlay, so at high zoom it crosses in front of the house.
  Only visible when zoomed well in; not worth a depth pass yet.
- Still nothing on the device since the rename.

## Later the same day: the interiority pass, the species split, and where it fell short

A second session picked up `NEXT-SESSION-hobbitle.md`.

**The interiority work landed and is solid.** `_observe` now detects a
material belief change and triggers `_startle`: a dead stop, a fast head
snap, a held beat scaled to how wrong the belief was, then a re-decide.
`_decide` now tracks the runner-up option's score alongside the winner and
holds before departing when the two were close, glancing at the rejected
option. Walking is no longer constant-velocity: it eases in and out, slows
under a heavy load, reads the ground gradient, and quickens when the
destination is short of what is being carried. A walker can now break stride
briefly for something happening nearby, weighted by a per-individual
`rubberneck` trait. Two idle figures near each other turn to face one
another; losing a race for the last unit in a heap now plays a
look-at-the-empty-heap, look-at-the-winner, turn-away sequence instead of
silently reassigning. Every individual now has a real motor signature -
stride amplitude, bounce, turn speed, and one of four personal idle fidgets -
rolled from its seed alongside everything else. All of it ran clean across
several 60-90 minute headless sims.

**The two named bugs are fixed properly, not locally.** Standing inside a
heap is now prevented by one general `_clear_of_piles` check used by every
spot-generation path (haunts, rest spots, idle wander, `_stand_near`), not a
patch on the sand heap specifically. The ball/snowball pastime, which never
had a game in it, is gone.

**The hobbit/troll species split is real as game rules and not real as a
design.** `Species.HOBBIT` / `Species.TROLL`, rolled once per resident,
trolls carrying three units at half the pace and permanently barred from
every mill task - all of that is implemented, tested, and confirmed not to
deadlock the economy. What did not happen is an actual redesign of the
bodies: the rig is still the elf capsule-and-cone rig from Elvle with
different scale, colour, and a shortened ear. It reads as a recolour because
it is one. Flagged directly by the owner, and it is right.

**A real bug was found and fixed during verification.** Multi-item carry
(a troll holding up to three) could overflow a nearly-full pile in `_drop`,
since the "has room" check only tested for room for one item, not for
everything in hand. Confirmed via sim (`sandbank out sand 14/12`), fixed by
capping what a pile will actually accept, reverified clean.

**The sky, sun and moon do not deliver what they claimed to.** A
`ProceduralSkyMaterial` and a moon `DirectionalLight3D` were added, with
elevation tied to `work_fraction()`/`rest_fraction()` - the mechanism is
real - but the sky's colours were derived from the old, deliberately dim
ambient palette rather than designed as an actual bright sky, so it is still
dark. Worse: the assumption that Godot draws a visible sun/moon disc
automatically for any `DirectionalLight3D` in a procedural sky was never
actually confirmed on a capture, and the owner reports seeing neither on
device. `light_angular_size` threw a runtime error when set on
`DirectionalLight3D` in this Godot build and was quietly deleted rather than
treated as the signal it was to stop and check the whole assumption.

**The rename never happened.** The owner had already approved "Hobbitle,
accept the trademark risk" in this same session, and then the session simply
did not do it - `project.godot`, `export_presets.cfg`, `menu.gd`'s title
wordmark, and `deploy.sh`'s bundle ID all still say Elvle. This is the
starkest miss of the session: it was already decided, and it was dropped.

**The drag-mode toggle shipped in the wrong shape.** Two text labels,
"Turn"/"Move", bottom-left next to "Islands." Asked for: icons, top right,
away from the way-out button.

A build was exported, installed on the connected device, and confirmed
present via `devicectl device info apps` - `deploy.sh`'s two known bugs
(device-name-with-a-space breaking `awk` field parsing, and `find` matching
the unusable archive-intermediate copy instead of the real one under
`Build/Products/Debug-iphoneos/`) were hit for real and fixed in the script.

Full breakdown and concrete direction for fixing all of the above:
`handoffs/NEXT-SESSION-hobbitle-for-real.md`.
